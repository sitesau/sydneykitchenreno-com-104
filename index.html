Kitchen Renovations Sydney Costs Explained

The cost of a kitchen renovation in Sydney is not a single number but a range that reflects the complexity of the project. Published guidance suggests most projects fall between about $15,000 and $100,000 plus. This wide spread exists because the final price depends heavily on the scope of work. A simple update to existing cabinetry and benchtops will sit at the lower end of the range. In contrast, a project that involves changing the layout, installing custom joinery, or selecting premium stone benchtops will push the total higher. Homeowners should also factor in the cost of high-end appliances and any specialised finishes. Site conditions play a significant role as well. If the property is a strata apartment, the builder may need to navigate body corporate approval processes, lift bookings and waste removal rules. Older homes often require heritage or structural work before the new kitchen can be installed. For more on kitchen renovations sydney, refer to the parent guide.

When comparing quotes, it is helpful to separate core building work from undecided appliance or finish choices. This makes it easier to see whether two proposals differ because of scope, product level or site conditions. A same footprint update sits in a different bracket from a project with layout changes. The biggest price drivers are layout changes, custom joinery, premium stone, appliance selection, strata or access constraints, and any heritage or structural work required before the new kitchen can be installed.

Timeline breakdown for Sydney projects

Understanding the timeline is just as important as the budget. A typical Sydney kitchen renovation takes around six to 12 weeks from sign-off to handover. This total period covers several distinct phases. The first phase involves design decisions, the preparation of the fixed price quote and the fabrication of cabinetry. Once the materials are ready, the project moves to the on-site phase, which usually lasts about two to four weeks. This on-site period includes demolition, cabinet installation, plumbing and electrical work, benchtop templating and the final fit off.

When comparing proposals, owners should ask for three practical dates instead of one headline promise. First, when the fixed price quote will arrive after the consult. Second, when cabinetry fabrication can be scheduled once selections are confirmed. Third, when the expected site start is, assuming approvals and access requirements are in place. This structure makes it easier to compare two offers that seem similar but rely on different assumptions. The total programme helps with move out, meal planning and temporary setup decisions. On site timing matters most for noise, access and day to day disruption. Apartment rules and access limits can affect the usable work window each day.

House type considerations

The type of property in Sydney significantly influences both the cost and the schedule. Heritage terraces, post-war brick homes and Federation style properties require careful joinery and finishes that respect the age of the house. Owners in these homes should ask what needs to be confirmed during measurement before cabinetry manufacture starts. Strata apartments present a different set of challenges. These projects often involve plumbing through party walls, acoustic underlay and after-hours work windows to minimise disturbance to neighbours. The builder must also handle body corp documentation and committee meetings.

For strata-approved apartment kitchens, the process includes body corp documentation, plumbing through party walls, acoustic underlay and after-hours work windows. The builder handles strata committees across Bondi Junction, Randwick and Alexandria. Because of these additional steps, apartment renovations often take longer to approve than standalone homes. If timing is critical, owners should ask the builder to separate the approval stage from the on-site stage in their timeline. This clarity helps identify where delays might occur and ensures the project stays on track.

The consultation and quoting process

The process begins with a free 60-minute in-home design consult. This meeting is most effective when the owner arrives with a clear brief rather than a loose wish list. The builder will take measurements and discuss layout options. To make the most of this time, owners should bring photos of the existing room, list storage needs and flag any non-negotiable layout requirements. The published process states that project details are reviewed within one business day. The builder then visits the home, measures the space and emails a fixed price quote within five to ten working days.

This quote should reflect the scope of work discussed during the consult. Owners should use this window to test whether the brief is complete enough for comparison. Ask if the layout is staying in place, whether plumbing or electrical points are moving, and which selections still need to be locked before the contract is prepared. The site work typically two to four weeks once cabinetry is on site. The builder handles the design, fixed price quote, home building contract and build itself end to end. A fixed price HIA or Master Builders contract is used, with a transparent variations process and NSW home building compensation cover included.
